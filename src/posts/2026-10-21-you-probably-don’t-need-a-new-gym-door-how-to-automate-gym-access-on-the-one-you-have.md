**Quick answer:** In most small gyms, you don’t have to replace your door to automate access. A locksmith can add an electric strike to your existing mechanical handle, and a controller tells it when to unlock. Panic bars, push paddles, and keys still open the door from the inside, so safety never depends on your software.

When independent owners hear “automated door access,” they picture ripping out a perfectly good door and paying for a full commercial install. Usually that isn’t the case, and it’s the biggest reason owners put the project off.

### Start With the Door You Have

Most small-town gyms already have a solid door with a mechanical handle. In many cases, a locksmith can add an electric strike to that existing setup. The strike is the small part in the frame that releases the latch when it gets a signal. Your handle stays your handle, which saves a lot of money and a lot of hassle compared with swapping out hardware.

I’d still have a locksmith look at your frame first. Some doors are easier than others.

### What the Controller and App Actually Do

The strike doesn’t decide anything on its own. At Kinect, the ReliaGate Controller does the thinking. A member opens the app on their phone, the system confirms they are on site and in good standing, and the controller tells the strike to release. No fobs, no shared door codes, and nobody has to let anyone in at 5 a.m.

That matters when you’re the one wearing every hat. Access follows the membership, so you aren’t managing a pile of keys and codes.

### Exits Always Win

Here’s what I tell every owner who worries about trapping people inside: the strike only controls entry. Manual keys, push paddles, and panic bars always override it, so anyone inside can leave at any time, whether the power is out or the software is down. Check exit requirements with your local fire and building inspector, since that’s not something to guess at.

### The Friday Night Bingo Test

At a community centre in rural Manitoba, the Friday night Bingo crew shows how simple this can be. When the group arrives, nobody opens the software. The panic bar gets disengaged so the door opens freely, and when the night is over it goes back on. No settings to change and no schedule to edit. That’s the kind of physical fallback a small operator should expect from any access system.

### FAQ

**Do I need to replace my door to add smart access?**
Usually not. A locksmith can often add an electric strike to your existing mechanical handle.

**Can people still exit during a power or internet outage?**
Yes. Keys, push paddles, and panic bars override the strike, so exits never depend on software.

**Can I open the door for an event without touching the software?**
Yes. Disengaging the panic bar lets people walk in freely, which works well for events like a Bingo night.
